# DESIGN CHALLENGE — "SCENES FROM A CITY THAT DOESN'T EXIST"

You are a graphic artist who works by ripping apart real pictures and gluing
them back together wrong on purpose. You find photos on the internet, cut
them up, recolor them, smash them into each other, and type big weird words
all over the top. You do not use AI to make the pictures. You steal them
fair and square and you make them your own.

Make me a series of six posters. Each poster is a single made-up scene that
looks like a photograph of a place that could never exist. Every poster is
built from THREE TO FIVE real photos you actually found, layered on top of
each other, with the colors changed so they all look like they belong in the
same broken world. Then you put type on it — big type, tiny type, type that
bends around things, type that is mostly just texture. Nothing stays clean.

This is not a tidy brand job. It should look like a magazine made by a ghost.
Rough, loud, layered, a little bit ugly in a beautiful way.

Here are the six posters. Make each one look different but make them all feel
like they came from the same haunted city.

---

**1. THE FLOOD THAT REMEMBERED**
A street underwater but the buildings are still standing and the signs are
still on. Find a real photo of a flooded city street. Find a real photo of an
old shop sign or a neon sign. Find a photo of a person standing waist-deep in
water. Cut the person out, make them small and lonely in the middle of the
flood. The sign should be huge and glow and reflect in the water. Recolor the
whole thing a cold green-blue so it feels like the water is also the sky.
Across the top in enormous letters, the words "THE FLOOD REMEMBERED" with the
last word half-sunk under the water line.

**2. THE MOON, PARKED ILLEGALLY**
A giant moon sitting in a parking lot like a car. Find a real photo of the
full moon. Find a real photo of an empty parking lot at night. Find a photo of
a parking meter or a ticket. Slap the moon right into a parking space. Give it
a license plate. Put a little yellow ticket on it. Make the sky grainy like a
bad photocopy. A tiny caption at the bottom that just says "TOWED AT DAWN" in
a stencil-ish look.

**3. THE FOREST THAT GREW THROUGH THE SUBWAY CAR**
An abandoned subway car with trees growing out of the windows. Find a real
photo of an old subway train. Find a real photo of a dense forest or a single
tree. Find a photo of moss or vines. Cut the trees so they burst out the
windows and up through the roof. Make the metal look rusted and the green look
too green. Put the station name on the side of the car but the name is a made
up word, and the type should look like old painted station lettering.

**4. THE MAN WHO ATE THE WEATHER**
A portrait but the face is made of a storm. Find a real photo of a person's
face, head on, serious. Find a real photo of a storm cloud or lightning.
Find a photo of rain on a window. Replace the person's head with the storm so
the lightning is their thoughts and the rain runs down their collar. Duotone
the whole thing hard — two colors only. The person's name and a made-up date
in small type in the corner, like a mugshot card.

**5. THE STAIRCASE THAT GOES UP AND ALSO DOWN**
A staircase that folds into itself like a maze. Find two or three real photos
of staircases from different buildings. Flip one upside down. Mirror one.
Smash them together so they connect but wrong, going in circles. Recolor to a
flat, hot, poster-like palette so it reads as a graphic, not a photo. Along
one edge, vertical text that runs the whole height of the poster, reading
"UP IS DOWN IS UP IS DOWN" over and over.

**6. THE CLOCK THAT COUNTED TOO FAST**
A room full of clocks that don't agree. Find a real photo of a wall of clocks
or a clock shop. Find a close-up of a single clock face. Find a photo of
hands or fingers. Duplicate the clock faces all over the image at different
sizes and angles, all showing different times. Make the hands blur or smear
so they look like they are spinning. One clock in the center, big and sharp,
showing a time that is impossible. Under it, tiny type: "the meeting is at
half past never."

---

## How to work

1. For every poster, actually go find the real photos. Do not use one photo
   and fake the rest. Search for each thing. Use more than one place to
   search. If a photo is hard to find, keep looking.
2. Cut, crop, recolor, blend, flip, mirror, duplicate. Make the layers obvious
   but make the scene believable in a broken way.
3. The type is part of the art, not a label. Big words, bent words, type as
   texture. Break the lines yourself so they wrap where you want.
4. Every time you render one, look at it. Read the words. If a word is cut
   off or a face looks wrong, fix it and render again.
5. Make them feel like a set. Same grime, same grain, same weirdness, but each
   poster its own idea.

## What I'm checking

- Did you use real found photos and change them a lot? (Not one stock image
  pasted flat.)
- Are there actually multiple layers doing work in each image?
- Does the type do something interesting, not just sit in a bar at the
  bottom?
- Do all six feel like the same city?

Go make a mess.
